Dataset: ecommerce_customer_data.csv — per-customer Recency (days since last purchase), Frequency (number of purchases), and Monetary (total spend) values.

5. Elbow Method — Choosing K¶

In [6]:
inertias = []
K_range = range(1, 11)
for k in K_range:
    km = KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init=10)
    km.fit(scaled)
    inertias.append(km.inertia_)

plt.figure()
plt.plot(list(K_range), inertias, marker="o")
plt.xlabel("Number of Clusters (K)")
plt.ylabel("Inertia (Within-Cluster Sum of Squares)")
plt.title("Elbow Method for Optimal K")
plt.xticks(list(K_range))
plt.show()

Observation: The elbow (the point where inertia stops dropping sharply) appears around K=4, matching the intuition that customers naturally fall into a handful of behavioural groups.

Interpreting the clusters (labels will match your actual cluster numbers — check the profile table above):

  • Champions — low Recency, high Frequency, high Monetary: your most valuable, most engaged customers.
  • Big spenders, lapsed — high Recency but historically high Monetary: valuable customers who haven't purchased recently.
  • Frequent, low-value — low Recency, high Frequency, low Monetary: loyal but small-basket shoppers.
  • At-risk / inactive — high Recency, low Frequency, low Monetary: customers close to churning.

10. Marketing Recommendations by Segment¶

  • Champions: Reward with loyalty perks and early access to new products — protect this high-value relationship.
  • Big spenders, lapsed: Run a targeted win-back campaign (personalised discount or "we miss you" email) before they churn entirely.
  • Frequent, low-value: Encourage bigger basket sizes with bundle deals or free-shipping thresholds.
  • At-risk / inactive: Re-engagement campaign with a strong incentive (e.g. first-purchase-style discount) or, if unresponsive, deprioritise marketing spend on this group.
